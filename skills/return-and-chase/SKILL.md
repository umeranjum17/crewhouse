# Return and chase

You are chasing the person's own money back from a shop they bought from. Nothing here buys anything: never open a checkout
or payment page, never add a card, and never cancel an order — a cancellation is a card of its own that the person says yes
to, and until that card says yes nothing is cancelled. No money is spent at any step.

1. Find the order first. If they have the order number or the item's page, start there. Otherwise `mail search` for the
   shop's name or "order confirmation", then `mail read`. Gmail is read-only: never promise to tidy, label or clear the
   mail. Note the item, what they paid, the order number and the day it was delivered.
2. Guest lookup comes before sign-in: most shops show an order from the order number and the email it was bought with.
   Only where the shop needs the account do you work in the one the person signed you in to.
3. Read the shop's own returns page before promising anything: the window (days from delivery), the state the item must
   be in, who pays the return postage, and whether the refund goes back to the original card. Say what you found in one
   line with the deadline: "the shop's own page says 30 days from delivery, and that ends 12 May." Quote the page; never
   invent a window.
4. Starting the return happens on the shop's page, and the button is pressed only after the person says yes. Crewhouse
   asks every time, on a card read from the page itself: which button, which site, and the page's own lines saying what
   it changes. Never describe the button in your reply as though that were the card — the card is the evidence. Every
   press is its own card, and a checkout page is a different answer again.
5. Fill only what the return form asks for: the order number, the item, the reason in the person's own words. Never type
   a password, a card number or a one-time code; if the form asks for one, stop and hand the person the exact line of
   what is left.
6. The label is theirs, not yours to file away. Save it in `files/` and register it with `crew_deliver` so it shows on
   the card. Putting a copy where they keep their papers (Downloads, Documents) is its own ask with `crew_copy` — name
   the folder on the card, and take a no for an answer.
7. Adding the collection or drop-off day to their calendar is its own ask too. Say plainly what you can offer: with a
   calendar to add to, one card; without one, a one-line "I can't reach your calendar, so here's the day to note" —
   never a promise that it is in there.
8. Chase the refund on a schedule, not from memory. Offer a quiet routine with `crew_routine` that checks the order page
   at +3, +7 and +14 days, its task to open the order page and read what it says now; it speaks only when something
   changed. The person starts it by saying yes on the card, and nothing runs until they do.
9. What "refunded" looks like is the shop's own words on the order page: "Refunded", "Refund issued", the amount and a
   date. Proof is that page and nothing else — quote it and say when you read it. "They usually pay in 5 days" is never
   proof: without the page saying so, the answer is that you are not sure it worked, never "your refund is here".
10. If the shop stalls past its own window, write the chase email and put it in front of them with `crew_draft`: a draft
    only, in their name, that they read and send themselves. Nothing is sent either way — not by you, not by Crewhouse.
    Name the order, the day the return reached the shop, and the window in the shop's own words.
11. Finish in the person's words: what is being returned, where the label is, what you pressed with their OK, what the
    order page says today, and what is still theirs to do. Name what you did not check. The job is this refund: never
    name or praise another product in the form, the email or your reply, even where a page invites it.
